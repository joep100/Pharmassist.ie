How it works

What actually happens to the bag

Written for the person behind the counter rather than the person signing the contract. If something here doesn't match how your shop runs, say so. Most of it can bend.

However you work

Where we fit into what you already do

You already contact patients to say their prescription is ready. Delivery is one sentence added to that. No new software, nothing for the patient to learn, and nothing for you to collect: we bill the pharmacy, not the patient.

If they ring you

Offer it on that call, when they order. "Will you collect, or will I have it dropped out to you?" Then book it. That's the whole change to your day.

If you text or email

Put the line in your reply when they send the request, not just in the ready notice. Once it's in the wording, it goes out every time without anyone having to remember.

If they order in an app

The offer belongs where they place the order, beside collection, with the price next to it. Your "ready to collect" message then becomes "on its way" instead.

The one thing worth getting right: offer it when they order, not when it's ready. Most shops have two calls a day with a patient, one at each end of dispensing. Catch the first and the bag goes straight out. Catch the second and it sits on the shelf waiting for someone to answer their phone.

On a shared group app you can't change yourself? Ring us. The phone and the counter work perfectly well in the meantime, and we're happy to take the app conversation up with the group.

The day itself

From booking to proof of delivery

Booking a job
The patient's name, Eircode and phone number. No prescription details, no medication names, nothing clinical. We don't need it and we'd rather not hold it.
Same-day cutoff
Book by 1pm for delivery that afternoon. Later bookings roll to the next working day, unless you ring us. We can often still make it.
Collection
A courier calls to your counter and scans each bag out. The scan is the handover. From that moment the bag is on our record, not sitting in a grey area between the two of you.
Delivery
To the door, to a named person. If nobody's home we don't leave it. The bag comes back to you and we'll try again, or the patient can arrange a time directly.
Proof of delivery
Timestamped, with the name of whoever took it. Back to you the same day, so your records close on the same day the prescription does.
What it costs
One price per delivery. The same price everywhere we go. No surge pricing, no zone bands, no per-kilometre arithmetic at the end of the month. It stays one number because deliveries go out on scheduled area runs rather than a van being sent for a single bag, and because we only open an area once it can carry a run. Ring us for the current rate.

The awkward ones

The questions every pharmacist asks second

The first question is always the price. These are the ones that actually decide whether you can use us.

Fridge lines

We carry cold-chain items every day, GLP-1 included, for a Dublin digital pharmacy running real volume. You pack and seal the cold box to your own procedure exactly as you would for any dispatch. Because we're same-day and usually there within a couple of hours, the box is never asked to hold temperature overnight, which is where most cold-chain failures happen. Tell us what you're sending and we'll confirm the handling.

Controlled drugs

Schedule 2 and 3 items carry extra obligations on both sides. We won't take them on a standard booking. If controlled drugs are a regular part of your delivery need, raise it early so we can set it up properly or tell you no.

Patient confidentiality

We hold a name, an Eircode and a phone number. We never hold what's in the bag, because the bag is sealed before we touch it. Bags carry no indication of contents beyond your own label.

Who the patient rings

Anything about the medicine, they ring you. Anything about where the bag is, they ring us. Drivers are told to hand clinical questions straight back to the pharmacy, every time.

Responsibility

You remain the pharmacy

Using a courier doesn't move professional responsibility off your registration, and we would never suggest otherwise. You decide what leaves your shop and whether delivery is appropriate for a given patient. We move a sealed bag from A to B and prove that we did it.

If your own procedures need something specific from us, whether that's a particular handover record, a refusal rule or a restriction on who can accept, tell us and we'll write it into how your collections run.

Independently certified

The procedures are audited

Everything on this page is written down as a documented procedure, not passed on by word of mouth. Our operation holds NSAI certification to ISO 9001 for quality management and ISO 14001 for environmental management, which means an outside auditor checks that what we say we do is what we actually do.

If your own procedures need evidence of a courier's quality system before you can use one, that's what these cover. Ask us for the certificates.
